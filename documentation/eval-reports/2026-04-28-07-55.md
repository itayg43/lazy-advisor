### allocation

**Rules alignment:** Mostly aligned. Key rules observed in practice:
- Split always stated in both shekels and percent
- Shekel amounts sum to exactly the investment amount in every run
- "tends to reduce" phrasing used consistently — no "prevents" or "eliminates"
- The words `conservative`, `moderate`, `aggressive` are never used with the user (confirmed by implementation: the prompt references `${RISK_LEVELS}` as a forbidden list, verified in implementation)
- Specific drawdown percentages appear only in sanity-check turns
- Counter-proposals are honored exactly (77%, 50/50 both verified)
- Extreme mismatches trigger a one-time sanity check then accept unconditionally

One philosophical tension exists in the prompt and rules: "The under 3 years column is 0–10% across all rows on purpose. Money needed in under 3 years is dominated by the need for it to still be there — **risk tolerance is not a meaningful dial at that horizon**." However, in the same breath, the prompt instructs the model to "pick one integer from the cell range based on qualitative signal (where the user sits within their risk bucket, how clean the timeline is)." In practice, the aggressive + under-3-years test produces 10% equity (top of 0–10% range), not a neutral 5%. This contradicts the stated philosophy that risk tolerance doesn't matter at this horizon. Technically within the cell, but inconsistent with the stated design principle.

**Prompt/rules sync:** The allocation implementation includes a `${RISK_LEVELS}` interpolation that injects the list of forbidden words directly into the prompt. This dynamic injection isn't documented in the rules file, which just says "the words conservative, moderate, and aggressive are never used." Minor — the rules capture the intent, not the mechanism.

**Conversation quality:** Generally strong. One quality issue: the sanity check for a long-horizon aggressive user asking for 0% equity includes the phrase "all-singles/higher-equity approach" — `all-singles` is meaningless in context (likely a model artifact or remnant phrase). Observed in last-run:
> "going to 0% stocks means your entire ₪50,000 stays in buffer, so you'd likely give up much of the long-run growth that stocks can provide—over many years that can mean materially lower outcomes versus an **all-singles/higher-equity approach**."

This doesn't affect the test outcome but is confusing phrasing that should be addressed in the prompt.

**Flow:** All paths complete within the 5-call budget. The counter-proposal + clarifying-question path (observed in "should handle a clarifying question followed by a counter-proposal") works correctly.

**Edge-case handling:** Well-covered across the three sanity-check variants (conservative → 100%, short-horizon → 100%, aggressive long-horizon → 0%). Missing coverage for:
- Sequential counter-proposals (user proposes 60%, then asks for 55% after the first confirmation)
- Budget exhaustion scenario (what happens at exactly 5 tool calls with no resolution)
